Why Nymo

Some things are easier to say to a stranger. No names, no profiles, no history. One person somewhere in the world listens. When one of you leaves, the chat is gone.

A few ground rules.

Everyone agrees to them before their first chat. They keep Nymo kind, anonymous and safe.

  • Stay anonymous

    Don’t share names, socials, numbers or where you live. Nymo hides them automatically.

  • Be kind

    Listen the way you’d want to be listened to. No judging, no pushing.

  • Leave anytime

    You never owe anyone a conversation. Report or block in one tap.

  • Not a crisis line

    Nymo is peer conversation, not professional help. In danger? Call your local emergency number.

Adults only. Age groups, never ages.

Nymo is for people 18 and older. The person you talk to sees your age group, never your exact age. Choose who you’d like to talk to; if nobody’s around after 30 seconds, Nymo widens the search to all ages, unless you’ve switched that off.

No ads, no data to sell and no paywall in front of a conversation. This is how Nymo pays for its servers.

Students join with a campus code. No names, no emails, no student IDs. They meet each other first and the rest of the world a few seconds later. The university sees monthly totals, like how many people talked and how they felt afterwards. Never a conversation.

Questions

The short answers about privacy, safety and how Nymo works.

Is Nymo really anonymous?

Yes. There’s no account, no email and no phone number. Nymo creates a random ID on your phone, and the person you talk to only sees “Stranger”, the topic and your age group.

What exactly gets hidden?

Names after phrases like “my name is”, places after “I live in” or “I’m from”, phone numbers, email addresses, links and @handles. They’re replaced with a gray bar before the message is saved.

The filter catches common patterns, not everything, so keep details that could identify you to yourself.

How long are chats kept?

Messages are deleted 60 minutes after a chat ends. A chat with no new messages for 30 minutes ends on its own. Nothing is saved on your phone, and chats are left out of device backups.

Can I talk to the same person again?

No. Once a chat closes it can’t be reopened, and there are no profiles to look anyone up. Each conversation stands on its own.

Who can use Nymo?

Adults, 18 and older. Nymo asks for your age group and doesn’t check IDs, so please be honest. If someone seems to be under 18, report the chat.

What if someone makes me uncomfortable?

End the chat with one tap. You can also report or block them. Reports are anonymous, and you won’t be matched with that person again.

Is Nymo therapy or a crisis service?

No. Nymo is conversation between peers. If you’re in danger, call your local emergency number. To talk to a trained listener, find a helpline in your country.

Can I delete my data?

Yes. Settings › Delete my data ends your chats and removes your ID, your block list and everything linked to it. Reports you’ve sent stay for safety, with only the random ID.

If you have Nymo Plus, the subscription itself keeps running in Google Play until you cancel it there.

Is Nymo free?

Yes. Every chat, the privacy filter and all safety tools are free, and they’ll stay that way. If you like, you can support Nymo once from inside the app. Optional extras may come later as Nymo Plus. There are no ads, and no data is sold.

Can my university see my chats?

No. A campus code only tells Nymo to look for someone from your campus first. Your university sees totals over a month, with small numbers left out: never names, never conversations.

The person you meet sees “Same campus”, so you might cross paths. Share accordingly.

Which phones does Nymo run on?

Android 8.0 or newer. There’s no iPhone version yet.
