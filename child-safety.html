Child safety standards

Nymo is an anonymous one-on-one chat app for adults. We do not tolerate child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE) of any kind. These are the standards we hold ourselves, and everyone on Nymo, to.

Zero tolerance

Nymo prohibits any content or behaviour that sexually exploits, abuses or endangers children. This includes:

  • child sexual abuse material (CSAM) of any kind, including drawn or computer-generated material, and links to it,
  • grooming: building trust with a minor in order to abuse or exploit them,
  • sexual conversations with anyone who is, or says they are, under 18,
  • sextortion, and asking anyone for sexual images,
  • trafficking and any other commercial sexual exploitation of children.

Anyone who breaks these rules is banned from Nymo. Where the law requires or allows it, we report them to the police.

Adults only

Nymo is only for people aged 18 and over. Before the first chat, everyone picks their age group. Choosing “Under 18” closes the app’s chat for good and points to help services for young people instead.

The person you talk to only ever sees an age group, never an exact age. We don’t check IDs, so anyone can report a chat as “Might be under 18”, and we look at these reports first.

How Nymo makes abuse harder

  • Text only. No photos, videos, voice messages or files can be sent.
  • Personal details are hidden automatically. Names, places, phone numbers, email addresses, links and social media handles are replaced with a gray bar before a message is delivered. That makes it hard to move a conversation somewhere else.
  • No profiles, no search, no friend lists. Nobody can be looked up, and a chat can’t be reopened once it ends.
  • Nothing lingers. Messages are deleted 60 minutes after a chat ends.
  • Blocking is permanent. Two people who blocked each other are never matched again.

Reporting in the app

Every chat has a Report button. It works without leaving the app, and the reasons include “Sexual content” and “Might be under 18”. When you report someone:

  • the chat ends right away, and you’re never matched with that person again,
  • your report keeps a copy of the last 100 (filtered) messages, so we can review it even after the chat itself has been deleted,
  • the other person isn’t told who reported them.

You can also write to us at support@example.com.

What we do with reports

  • We review reports as quickly as we can, and reports about sexual content or minors first.
  • When a report shows a risk to a child, we ban the reported account at once: their chats end, they can no longer search, and their sign-in is blocked.
  • We keep the evidence the authorities need, and only the people who review reports can see it.
  • We never pass abusive material on to anyone except the authorities.

Working with the authorities

When we learn of child sexual abuse material or a child at risk, we report it to the police. In Austria that is the reporting office for child sexual abuse material at the Federal Criminal Police Office (Bundeskriminalamt). Content hosted elsewhere goes through Stopline, the Austrian member of the INHOPE network of hotlines.

We respond to lawful requests from law enforcement and comply with the child safety laws that apply to us, including those of the European Union.

If you need help

A child is in danger right now?

Call the police: 112 across the European Union, 133 in Austria.

  • Found child sexual abuse material online? Report it anonymously at stopline.at (Austria) or to the hotline in your country listed at inhope.org. Don’t download, save or share it, not even as evidence.
  • For children and young people: Rat auf Draht, 147 in Austria, free and around the clock. In many European countries, 116 111 reaches a child helpline. Or find one at findahelpline.com.

Contact

Questions about these standards, or a report you’d rather send by email: support@example.com. Our child safety contact can answer questions about how Nymo prevents child sexual abuse material and keeps to these standards.
